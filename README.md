# Graduate Employment & Earnings Outcomes: Exploratory Analysis (Excel)

Exploratory analysis of employment and earnings outcomes for recent graduates across 173 college majors (FiveThirtyEight / U.S. Census ACS data), framed as a program-evaluation case for university leadership.

## Key findings

1. **Pay tracks gender composition.** Share of women in a major correlates −0.62 with median salary; predominantly female majors earn a median of $33K vs. $50K for predominantly male majors.
2. **The gap is in pay, not job access.** Unemployment is similar across both groups (6.9% vs. 6.2%).
3. **The extremes are lopsided.** Women make up 20% of graduates in the highest-paying majors and 79% in the lowest-paying majors.

## Methods

PivotTables, XLOOKUP, summary statistics, and conditional-formatting heatmaps to compare salaries, unemployment, part-time work, and low-wage and non-college employment across majors and major categories.

## Recommendations

- Build internship pipelines for majors with above-average unemployment.
- Offer career-transition support for graduates in lower-paying fields.
- Start early outreach to widen access to higher-earning pathways.
